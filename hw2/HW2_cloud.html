function solve_allocation(w, K)
    model = Model(Gurobi.Optimizer)
    set_silent(model)

    @variable(model, x[1:n, 1:m] >= 0)
    @constraint(model, assign[i = 1:n], sum(x[i, j] for j in 1:m) == 1)
    @constraint(model, machine[j = 1:m], sum(w[i, j] * x[i, j] for i in 1:n) <= K[j])
    @objective(model, Min, sum(c[i, j] * x[i, j] for i in 1:n, j in 1:m))

    optimize!(model)
    @assert termination_status(model) == MOI.OPTIMAL
    return (obj = objective_value(model), X = value.(x), q = dual.(machine), p = dual.(assign))
end

The optimal energy consumption is $41{,}416.51$. If machine capacities were ignored, the minimum energy consumption would be $40{,}883.16$. Thus, the capacity constraints increase total energy consumption by $533.35$, or about $1.3\%$.

In the optimal solution, $386$ of the $1000$ jobs are not assigned to their lowest-energy machine. This happens because some of the machines that are best for many jobs do not have enough capacity to process all of them, so some jobs must be assigned to their second- or third-best machines.

Also, since $ \sum_{j=1}^{20} C_j=1000, $ which is exactly the total number of jobs, every machine must be used at full capacity. The solution is integral, so no job is split across multiple machines, which is consistent with the network-flow integrality result from part (a).

The optimal energy consumption is $46{,}693.58$. If the utilization constraints were ignored, the minimum energy consumption would be $40{,}883.16$. Thus, the utilization constraints increase total energy consumption by $5{,}810.42$, or about $14.2\%$.

In the optimal solution, $885$ of the $1000$ jobs are not fully assigned to their lowest-energy machine. This is because the utilization constraints are more restrictive than the simple capacity constraints in part (b). We have different jobs use different amounts of utilization on different machines.

Unlike part (b), the optimal solution is not integral. In this solution, $20$ jobs are split across multiple machines. All $20$ machines also operate at their maximum utilization.

Comments.

All of the dual variables are negative, ranging from approximately $-1.06$ to $-0.41$. Since each $q_j<0$, complementary slackness implies that all of the machine utilization constraints are binding.

The magnitude of $q_j$ represents the value of additional utilization capacity. For example, a dual value of $-1.06$ means that increasing the maximal utilization of that machine by one unit would reduce the optimal energy consumption by approximately $1.06$ units.

From the scatter plot, machines with smaller maximal utilization tend to have more negative dual values, while machines with larger maximal utilization tend to have dual values closer to zero. Thus, additional utilization appears to be more valuable for machines with tighter utilization limits.
